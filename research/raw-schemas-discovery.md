# Serial path-schema discovery

Implemented in `src/raw_schemas.rs`; exact checking belongs to `src/raw_schema.rs`.
Discovery emits candidate serial sublanguages and never asserts that their union
is the entire automaton Parikh image.

## Construction

For each visited anchor, breadth-first paths to and from that anchor yield
closed walks `anchor -> edge.source -> edge.target -> anchor`. Include the
anchor's self-loops directly. Self-loops at other states are deferred until
those anchors are visited; folding them into return tours adds many weak
compound periods. Every retained closed walk is an explicit sequence of original
serial-automaton edge IDs.

Explore finite skeleton walks breadth first from the initial state. A schema
can retain closed walks at every visited anchor, with independently repeated
cycles. Returning to an earlier anchor remains permitted: the intervening
segment may introduce new independent periods. This is necessary even for two
states with an output self-loop available only away from the initial state.

Normalize period sets by retaining only generators not already expressed as
nonnegative combinations of shorter retained generators. This is a bounded
exact search: only a completed decomposition allows removal. An incomplete
search retains the generator. At an identical endpoint and normalized period
set, discard a new base only when its difference from an earlier base is
constructively generated by those periods. Candidate schemas with identical
bases and period vectors are emitted once, even if their endpoints differ.

Every skeleton and cycle retains its actual edge path. The certificate checker
reconstructs vectors and validates path continuity, closed cycles and accepting
endpoints independently. Resource limits can shorten candidate discovery;
they do not establish nonmembership or a negative reachability answer.

## Structural evidence

Read directly from the six unresolved development queries in
`benchmarks/raw-diverse-automaton-v1`:

| Input | Total states | Reachable serial states | Reachable edges | Self-loops |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| optimistic_v3_validated | 6 | 6 | 12 | 6 |
| optimistic_v5_validated | 10 | 10 | 20 | 10 |
| optimistic_v9_validated | 18 | 18 | 36 | 18 |
| write_skew_n3_pairlocked | 64 | 7 | 28 | 16 |
| write_skew_n4_pairlocked | 256 | 15 | 75 | 45 |
| write_skew_n5_pairlocked | 1024 | 31 | 186 | 116 |

These small reachable automata support trying path schemas despite large raw
Petri nets (the largest source query is 42,373,976 JSON bytes). A structural
Python prototype reached a finite dominance closure at 6, 28, 125 and 571
endpoint/base/period combinations for optimistic_v3 and the three pairlocked
sizes respectively. Those prototype counts are **not solver results** and are
not a guarantee that the Rust candidate budget or the component-invariant
search will prove these inputs. The component invariant's control projection
may independently become too large.

The implementation uses only graph structure and response-place IDs, with no
source names or benchmark-family recognition. Source-level safety expectations
remain separate from mechanically checked negative results.

Four Rust discovery tests pass (cycle residues, returning to earlier anchors,
independently checking generated vectors on small NFAs, and resource/malformed
input behavior). Checked schema wrappers and CLI round trips also pass.
No measured negative result is claimed here.
