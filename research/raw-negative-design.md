# Direct raw negative proofs by controller-indexed semilinear invariants

## Status and recommendation

**Proposal, not an implemented or mechanically verified raw negative procedure.** The current raw methods cannot certify a negative answer: `raw_search::solve` explicitly returns unknown on exhaustion; `raw_target` decides membership of one supplied marking; `raw_potential` searches sufficient nonmembership goals and ignores negative subresults. A universal inclusion proof is missing.

Implement a certificate for a **controller-indexed semilinear invariant of credited responses**. It is a general net-level certificate, with no program-name or lock-name condition. A finite controller tracks bounded parts of the original marking. A fixed integer linear map credits irrevocably determined pending responses before their eventual emission. A finite union of linear sets at each controller state overapproximates all credited response vectors. Transition closure and final inclusion into the supplied serial union are verified constructively, using affine maps between nonnegative generator coefficients.

This gives a particularly small candidate proof for `counter_d3_s1_locked`, derived below from its original 11,066-byte query. The 34,415-byte `replicas_n2_locked` query has a similar plausible structure, but no complete candidate proof is derived here. No large query, solver, test, build, or timing experiment was run for this audit.

## Exact semantics and why pointwise LP is insufficient

Let `Z` be the original completion-zero places, `R` the response coordinates, and

`S = union_j (b_j + B_j N^{k_j})`

the serial set supplied in the raw query. We must prove every reachable marking `m` with `m[Z]=0` satisfies `m[R] in S`. The solver must not infer this from finding some nonnegative coefficients for one sampled response vector. It must not replace integer coefficients by rational coefficients, or replace a monoid by its rational cone and a lattice. For example, 1 is in the nonnegative rational cone and integer lattice generated by 2 and 3, but not their nonnegative integer monoid.

The construction below provides a symbolic coefficient function for **every** parameter valuation represented by the invariant. The checker verifies the function's domain, integrality, nonnegativity, and exact vector identity. This is the missing universal quantifier.

## Certificate construction and proof obligations

### 1. Finite projected controller

Choose original place indices `C` and provide a finite list `Q` of exact markings of `C`. The checker reconstructs projected transitions directly from original pre/post arcs, dropping guards outside `C`. It checks:

- The projection of the original initial marking occurs in `Q`.
- For every `q in Q` and every original transition enabled on the selected coordinates, its exact projected successor is also in `Q`.
- All state values, arcs, and updates are natural numbers; arithmetic is exact and overflow is a rejection.

Every original run projects into this graph, by induction. No separate claim that all listed controller states are reachable is necessary. All original transitions must be considered, including stuttering ones. In particular a response-producing transition cannot be discarded merely because its projection on `C` is a self-loop.

This generalizes the current one-token controller: several bounded token coordinates can be tracked together. The discovery procedure may fail to find a finite controller, in which case it returns unknown. An arbitrary finite sample of controller states is not a certificate.

### 2. Credited responses

Provide an integer matrix `W` with one row per response and one column per original place, and define `y = W m`. Require:

- `W[:,R]` is the identity in the original response order.
- Every column outside `R union Z` is zero.
- In the first implementation, require all entries of `W` nonnegative.

Consequently `y=m[R]` at every completed marking. The values assigned to `Z` are a proposed invariant representation; they are not trusted semantic assertions about the source program. If the choices are inappropriate, the following closure obligations fail.

For an original transition `t`, compute its credited effect exactly as `a_t = W (post_t-pre_t)`. This can be signed even with nonnegative `W`. Every original step satisfies `y'=y+a_t`. The checker must use original incidence, not a producer's claimed effect.

The benefit is that a transition moving a terminal local token into its predetermined response can have credited effect zero. Arbitrarily many pending response emissions then cease to enlarge the finite controller.

### 3. Semilinear invariant and initial membership

At each `q in Q`, provide finitely many pieces

`J_q = union_i (c_{q,i} + D_{q,i} N^{h_{q,i}})`.

Use nonnegative integer bases and periods initially. The intended invariant is:

`m[C]=q  ==>  Wm in J_q` for every reachable `m`.

For the initial marking provide a piece index and explicit natural coefficient vector `z0` satisfying `Wm0 = c + D z0`. Check the vector equality exactly.

### 4. Universal transition closure through generator maps

For every graph edge `(q,t,q')` and every source piece `c+D N^h`, provide a destination piece `c'+D' N^{h'}` and an affine integer map `z' = v + H z`. A simple sufficient certificate checks:

`c + a_t = c' + D' v`,

`D = D' H`,

`v >= 0`, `H >= 0`, all integer.

These finite arithmetic checks prove `c+D z+a_t in J_q'` for **all** natural `z`. They also address correlated periods: every source period is mapped to an actual nonnegative integer combination of destination periods. The source piece may contain unreachable values; closure must hold for those values too. This deliberate strength makes the checker small but the discovery procedure incomplete.

If one destination piece cannot cover a whole source piece, allow a finite, exhaustive decision tree over its integer parameters. A node splits on `a.z <= k` and `a.z >= k+1`. At each leaf choose a destination piece and an integer affine map, then certify nonnegativity throughout that leaf. Checking a subset of sampled parameter values is insufficient. Coverage follows syntactically from the complementary integer branches, without complementing `S`.

### 5. Completed-response inclusion

Only controller states whose selected coordinates in `Z` are zero can represent a completed marking. For every piece at each such state, provide the same kind of affine-map certificate into a **named original component** of `S`:

`c + D z = b_j + B_j (v+H z)` for all parameters of that leaf,

`v+H z` is nonnegative and integer there.

One source piece may require several exhaustive parameter cells because the input serial union contains finite prefixes and separate eventual components. The checker uses the original serial bases and periods, preserving their correlations. It does not accept a replacement union without separately checked inclusion.

### 6. Leaf obligations and trusted arithmetic

Start with denominator-one integer affine maps. For unrestricted `z>=0`, coefficientwise nonnegative `v,H` suffices. For a guarded leaf, rational Farkas certificates can establish universal linear inequalities (nonnegativity of every output coefficient). Check vector equalities coefficientwise when possible; otherwise check both inequality directions over the leaf. Infeasible leaves require checked infeasibility certificates. Integer tree coverage remains valid even when the Farkas proof overapproximates each leaf by its rational polyhedron.

Rational affine maps require additional exact divisibility certificates, and are a later extension. Rational feasibility alone does not prove the image coefficients are integers. Smith/Hermite normal forms or explicit residue splits could discharge divisibility, but no such implementation exists here.

### Resulting theorem

Initial membership and edge closure imply every reachable credited response lies in its controller invariant. At completion the credited response is the actual response; final piece inclusion therefore places it in the supplied serial set. Thus the original `completed-outside-semilinear` target is unreachable. Nothing in this argument constructs the complement or disjunctive normal form of the serial set.

## Suggested certificate shape

```text
kind: raw-semilinear-invariant-v1
controller:
  places: sorted original place indices
  states: finite exact projected markings
credit: sparse nonnegative integer response-by-original-place matrix
pieces: per controller state [{base, periods}]
initial: {piece, coefficients}
steps:
  one entry per checker-reconstructed controller edge and source piece
  {destination_piece, base_coefficients, period_coefficients}
  or an exhaustive integer split tree with guarded affine-map leaves
completed:
  one entry per completion-compatible state and source piece
  {original_serial_component, affine_generator_map, nonnegativity_proofs}
  or an exhaustive integer split tree
```

Matrices should use arbitrary-precision integer strings or checked bounded integers. The checker rejects missing edges/pieces, invalid dimensions, stale component indices, nonexhaustive split forms, missing initial membership, invalid generator maps, and unknown proof kinds. No proof obligation may be silently skipped on a timeout. An independent Python checker can reconstruct all projected edges and perform these identities directly.

The simplest first version uses unrestricted nonnegative transition maps and split-tree membership leaves. This already represents the candidate below. More general parameter splits on transition edges can be deferred.

## Concrete small counter candidate from the original query

The following is a manually derived candidate, not an implemented proof result. It uses numeric indices only to describe this specific inspected diagnostic; automatic discovery must be structural.

For `counter_d3_s1_locked`, response order is `(r0,r1,r2)` at original places `(13,14,15)`. Completion places are `0..6`. Choose credits

`y0 = m13 + m4`, `y1 = m14 + m1`, `y2 = m15 + m2`.

The three response-emission transitions `t1,t2,t3` then have zero credited effect by direct incidence arithmetic. Place 0 is the unbounded request queue, and places 1,2,4 are pending responses; neither belongs in the finite controller.

Select `C={3,5,6,7,8,9,10,11,12}`. A candidate finite controller has six states, written by their nonzero unit places:

- `A={7}`; `A_busy={6,8}`.
- `B={11}`; `B_busy={3,9}`.
- `C={12}`; `C_busy={5,10}`.

Reading the original 28 transitions gives the controller-changing cycle

`A --t4--> A_busy --t5--> B --t7--> B_busy --t21--> C --t10--> C_busy --t15--> A`.

Request creation, response emission, and lock-wait steps are projected self-loops. Other compiled transitions require incompatible selected local/global tokens and are disabled in these six states. A future checker must verify all 6 x 28 transition obligations rather than trusting this description.

Let `u=(1,1,1)`, `e1=(0,1,0)`, `e2=(0,0,1)`. Propose:

- `J_A=J_A_busy = {n u : n>=0}`.
- `J_B=J_B_busy = {n u+e1 : n>=0}`.
- `J_C=J_C_busy = {n u+e1+e2 : n>=0}`.

`t5` adds `e1`; `t21` adds `e2`; `t15` adds `e0`. Every step map preserves `n`, except `t15` sends `n` to `n+1`. Other credited effects in enabled edges are zero. Initial credited response is zero. Busy states cannot be completed because they contain a selected completion-place token.

The raw serial union contains eight components, in original order. Directly inspecting their bases and periods gives these inclusion leaves:

| Invariant piece | Parameter cell | Original serial component | Its period coefficient |
|---|---|---:|---|
| `n u` | `n=0` | 0 | no periods |
| `n u` | `n=1` | 3 | no periods |
| `n u` | `n>=2` | 6, base `2u`, period `u` | `n-2` |
| `n u+e1` | `n=0` | 1 | no periods |
| `n u+e1` | `n=1` | 4 | no periods |
| `n u+e1` | `n>=2` | 7, base `2u+e1`, period `u` | `n-2` |
| `n u+e1+e2` | `n=0` | 2 | no periods |
| `n u+e1+e2` | `n>=1` | 5, base `u+e1+e2`, period `u` | `n-1` |

These are exact identities and nonnegative integer witness formulas on exhaustive natural-number cells. In particular the base/period structure of the *actual supplied union* is sufficient; no complement or semantic simplification is needed. The remaining work is implementing and independently checking the controller closure and the full certificate, then discovering it automatically without this manually chosen controller/credit map.

## Discovery strategy and limits

1. Identify conserved global-state coordinates using `control::discover`, then refine with local coordinates when dropping their enabling guards introduces edges inconsistent with a candidate invariant. A one-token global controller alone is too coarse for the counter: e.g. `t6` can spuriously finish a request at the wrong global state if its local-stage guard is dropped. A finite exact closure on the larger projection certifies the refinement. Failure to establish finiteness returns unknown.
2. Propose credits by backward propagation from response-only completion transitions, or solve integer linear equations `W delta_t=0` for candidate response-flush transitions, with response identity and support restricted to `R union Z`. These equations only propose a representation; full closure proves correctness. Avoid source-name recognition.
3. Use weighted graph paths/cycles and the supplied serial periods to propose bases and periods at controller states. Check every edge with explicit nonnegative generator maps. Cycles suggest generators but do not by themselves prove an inductive cover: check noncycle edges and every invariant piece.
4. Try inclusion in each original serial component through exact integer linear algebra and generator maps. On failure, split a generator parameter at a small constant suggested by input bases. The small counter requires only cutpoints 0 and 1. General parameters may require multidimensional or residue cells; uncontrolled splitting can be expensive.
5. Only after this certificate works on the small counter, attempt replicas. Its inspected input has 34 places, 76 transitions, 24 serial components and 56 listed periods. Responses 1 and 2 occur in the raw response coordinates but are absent from the serial vectors. Therefore any negative proof must establish they cannot occur in completed executions, not silently delete those response dimensions. Reads can occur unboundedly in either coherent data state; writes alternate. This suggests unit read generators plus correlated write-cycle generators, but their mixed prefix/cycle conditions still require a checked invariant. No complete replica certificate is established here.

This is an incomplete proof search, not a complete decision procedure for arbitrary raw reachability. Reachability sets need not be semilinear, finite projections may be too weak, and uniform affine generator maps can fail even for a valid inclusion. An unknown result is appropriate. The merit to test is a reusable direct contract certificate with a small checker, rather than claiming universal existential LP synthesis solves arbitrary semilinear inclusion.

## Nearest infrastructure and practical scope

- `raw_target.rs`: validated original contract data, concrete membership, completion/response classifications. Extend the raw CLI negative-proof dispatch; it currently verifies only reachable raw answers.
- `control.rs`: conserved one-token controllers and original transition indices. Extend to finite projected closures; do not reuse projection transition deduplication in a way that loses credited effects.
- `projection.rs`, `cegar.rs`, `local_closure.rs`: original-arc projection and independent finite-closure checking patterns. Threshold abstractions can suggest bounded coordinates, but the proposed first controller certificate uses exact finite states.
- `linear.rs`: exact checked Farkas infrastructure for affine-map nonnegativity on split leaves. It is a checker component, not evidence that the universal relation is automatically synthesizable.
- `token_cut.rs` / `token_flow.rs`: alternative route from completed-execution summaries to piecewise affine generator witnesses, as Pro proposed. This needs universal membership coverage for all integer solutions of the relaxation and can inherit its SCC weakness. The controller-indexed invariant instead checks stepwise closure, which directly addresses the reachable local/global correlation in the inspected counter.

Novelty is unestablished. This combines established finite abstraction, semilinear inductive invariants, credit/potential maps, and constructive affine membership witnesses. A publication claim requires literature comparison and measured benefits over the existing complement-based SER workflow, not merely a successful locked diagnostic.
