# Independent lazy token-flow checker

`lazy_token_cut_checker.py` validates `lazy-finite-token-cut-v1` certificates
without trusting active-column identities. It validates original weighted nets
and potential bounds, reconstructs canonical BFS modes, and checks exact rational
Farkas coefficients for every eligible original edge, including implicit stutters.
All terminal modes and final-marking columns must be covered. Missing modes,
unbounded outgoing cuts, malformed multipliers and exhausted work reject.

The checker stores modes and projected transition guards but never the full
edge matrix. Its work limit is 20 million charged steps across reconstruction
and column checking; this may reject large proofs more conservatively than the
Rust solver's graph-visit limit. Validation is separate from solver timing and
must retain such limit failures.

Seven test groups pass normally and under Python `-O`: existing finite negative
proofs and dispatch; a spurious restricted refutation invalidated by an omitted
producing stutter; 8,200 stutters beyond the legacy explicit cap; canonical-mode
equality across every coordinate subset; streaming coefficients versus the full
explicit matrix; rejection of an unbounded outgoing cut even at zero multiplier;
malformed/incomplete proofs and work exhaustion. All 26 token-cut checker tests
also pass. No assertions are used for certificate acceptance.

Rust implementation and cross-language generated-answer checks are still pending.
No benchmark gain or publication claim follows from these checker tests.

The existing weighted-cycle finite proof also passes the new streaming checker
normally and under `-O` after changing only its proof kind. Saved as
`lazy-token-cut-example-answer.json`; this is a converted legacy certificate,
not an answer generated by the new Rust engine. It exercises the nonempty
finite projection and bounded cuts against the full original edge family.

Actual release-generated answers now pass independent Python checks normally and
under `-O` in `results/lazy-token-cut-crosscheck-v1`: a relevance-wrapped negative,
a replayed positive, and the weighted cycle. The cycle proof reports three modes,
three fixed edges, one active of two implicit stutters, four pricing rounds and
one cut. This exercises an actual implicit-column refutation, not just a converted
legacy certificate. Its solver reason is diagnostic output; acceptance comes from
the independent proof checker.
